# Writing style

Condensed from the `writing-clearly-and-concisely` skill in [softaworks/agent-toolkit](https://github.com/softaworks/agent-toolkit/tree/main/skills/writing-clearly-and-concisely) (MIT), which summarises William Strunk Jr.'s *The Elements of Style*.

## Composition

- **Use active voice.** "The installer writes the index", not "The index is written by the installer".
- **Put statements in positive form.** "Skips existing files", not "Does not overwrite files that already exist".
- **Use definite, specific, concrete language.** Name the file, the command, the number.
- **Omit needless words.** Cut "in order to", "it should be noted that", "basically", "simply".
- **Keep related words together.** Put a modifier next to the word it modifies.
- **Place the emphatic words at the end of the sentence.**
- One paragraph per topic, opening with its topic sentence.
- Express parallel ideas in parallel form, especially in lists.
- Keep to one tense in summaries.

## Usage

- Use a comma after each item in a series except the last: "context, prompts, and skills".
- Don't join independent clauses with a comma; use a period or a semicolon.
- A participial phrase at the start of a sentence refers to the grammatical subject.

## Patterns to avoid

- **Puffery:** pivotal, crucial, vital, testament, enduring legacy.
- **Empty "-ing" phrases:** ensuring reliability, showcasing features, highlighting capabilities.
- **Promotional adjectives:** groundbreaking, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, powerful.
- **Overused AI vocabulary:** delve, leverage, multifaceted, foster, realm, tapestry.
- **Formatting overuse:** bullets for what reads better as a sentence, emoji, bold on every other phrase.

Be specific, not grandiose. Say what the thing does.
